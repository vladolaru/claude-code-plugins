# image-optimizer design record

Why `imgopt.py` decides the way it does. Read this before changing a profile floor, the pick or verdict rules, what counts as lossless, or the tooling policy. The code's module docstrings hold the mechanics; this file holds the reasons and the evidence behind them.

## Where 2.0 came from

1.2.0 ran one lossless pass per folder through the `imageoptim` CLI, which is x86_64-only. A WooCommerce asset session in October 2026 (PRs #69539, #69553 and #69556) needed what that pass could not give: per-file lossy picks under quality floors, resizing to the display size, real metrics, a banding check, a 1:1 visual review and evidence a reviewer can reproduce. Every mechanical mistake in that session (comparing alpha images without flattening them, a parser that only read JPEG output, Pillow's PNG optimizer used as if it were oxipng, downscaled contact sheets that hid banding) became a rule the scripts own. Judgment stays with the agent (where an image is used, how large it is shown, which profile) and approval with the human (any change to pixels).

## Profiles and their floors

| Profile | SSIM | ssimulacra2 | Banding |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lossless` | decoded pixels identical, colour profile and orientation kept | | |
| `high` | ≥ 0.98 | ≥ 80 | ≤ 3 |
| `medium` | ≥ 0.96 | ≥ 60 | ≤ 3 |

These are not validated constants, and nobody should treat them as such:

- **SSIM 0.98** is the floor the maintainer set after rejecting a PR whose files measured 0.922 to 0.988. **0.96** is the floor set for files kept only so old URLs keep working.
- **ssimulacra2 80 and 60** were chosen during the session and confirmed by eye. They follow ssimulacra2's own scale, where 70–90 is high quality and 50–70 is medium.
- **Both metrics stay** because each caught a candidate the other passed.
- **What they were tested on:** a wider set than the one that set them (next item): 32 small PNGs (median 7 KB), 3 survey JPEGs, 16 photos in use and 17 orphaned photos. No WebP, AVIF or GIF output was ever gated, which is why WebP and AVIF output is labelled uncalibrated and every crop of it must be viewed.
- **Calibrated in one session.** The floors were set on the four photos of #69539 and the thirteen PNGs of #69553 and #69556, all WooCommerce assets; the wider set above is what they were tested on, not what set them. The benchmark corpus (386 files, 2026-10-10) then tested them on more kinds of images: a reviewer accepted 94 of 97 lossy picks sampled closest to the floors. The three rejections (two dark, flat illustrations converted to JPEG and one grayscale photo) were too few to justify a per-category floor, so both profiles stay as they are.
- **No margin above the floor.** The smallest passing candidate wins, so a pick sits at the floor, where the reported-only metrics (butteraugli, banding outside palette PNGs) look worst: the `gallery-6` test photo's pick under `high` measured SSIM 0.9827 with banding 5.0. A size margin would move every pick; the corpus review accepted picks at the floor, so none was added. The page shows each gate beside the reported value.

SSIM is ffmpeg's `ssim` on gray (luma only), computed on both images flattened onto white, and also onto dark grey when the image has alpha; the worse score counts. Comparing alpha images without flattening them produced 0.74 for a pair that measured 0.965 once flattened. Gray is compared on a pedestal of 4 levels (in 16 bits, so no two levels merge), because ffmpeg's `ssim` uses a luminance constant 1/64 of the published one: near black, a window of 0s against one of 1s scored 0.09 instead of 0.87. A JPEG whose luma DC step leaves black on a rounding tie decodes black as 1 in libjpeg-turbo, so a whole black product background failed a rung that ffmpeg's own decode scored 0.98 (SSIM 0.42 at ssimulacra2 76; 155 of 16,939 benchmark candidates, 2026-10-12). With the pedestal those score 0.96 to 0.99, ordinary content between 0.95 and 0.99 moves by +0.0003 (median) to +0.0013, and the floors were not re-calibrated. The pedestal ends the collapse, not every rejection: flat black against black lifted one level scores 0.976, under the `high` floor, and the product photo above scored 0.967. A larger pedestal does not close that gap (at 8 the worst benchmark case was still 0.971) and doubles the shift on ordinary content; the lift is a real one-level change across the frame, and those files mostly fail ssimulacra2's `high` floor too (the product photo: 75.9). The reviewer one-liner uses the same gray preparation.

**Banding is gated only on palette PNG output.** That is the one case calibrated against a real failure: #69556, a 24-colour gradient cut to 16 colours, which passed both other metrics. Lossy photo encodes scored 4 to 14 without visible banding, and the WebP of the #69556 gradient header scored 6. A threshold for those formats needs its own calibration, so their score is reported, not gated, and the smooth-area crop is required viewing instead. The benchmark corpus confirmed this for JPEG and WebP: accepted picks reached 11 to 14 under `medium` JPEG and 24 on a WebP screenshot, while the three rejected picks scored 4, so no threshold separates them.

**The Evidence SSIM is held to the floor too.** The Evidence SSIM is the number a PR reviewer gets from the ffmpeg one-liner `compare` prints. The gate decodes with Pillow, while that one-liner runs ffmpeg, whose JPEG decoder differs by up to about 1e-3 (9.8e-4 on the #69539 photos). `candidates` therefore runs the reviewer check on each lossy pick. A pick whose Evidence SSIM falls below the floor gives way to the next passing candidate.

**The Evidence SSIM is a luma SSIM.** It does not see colour: a copy of a 4 MP photo with its luma kept and its chroma blurred and shifted scored 0.995 against ssimulacra2 7.5. The ssimulacra2 floor rejects such a copy, so the evidence table puts ssimulacra2 beside the luma SSIM, and every output labels the number "Evidence SSIM (luma)".

**Reproduction depends on the ffmpeg build.** With the same ffmpeg build the reviewer one-liner prints the same number (see Acceptance evidence). Across builds a different IDCT or scaler can move the last digits; the skill tells reviewers to expect agreement to about 1e-4, an estimate that no second build has tested. `compare` prints the ffmpeg version beside the command so a mismatch can be traced to the build.

## What counts as lossless

A pick is lossless only when a lossless encoder rewrote the source file itself. Then its colour profile and orientation survive and it needs no approval. Anything re-encoded from prepared pixels is lossy, even when its encoder is lossless: pixels converted to sRGB, rotated by their orientation tag, resized or flattened. Such a pick needs crops and the human's approval. Before this rule, a Display P3 PNG under `high` was reported as "identical", and `apply` overwrote it without its profile and with its wide-gamut colours clipped. A pick made from pixels carries no colour profile, since its pixels are sRGB: the profile Pillow attaches when converting holds the time it was made, and cjpeg and oxipng copied it, so the same input gave different bytes on every run.

Pillow's decoded pixels are not everything a browser or screen reader sees, so "identical" also covers:

- **PNG colour chunks.** Pillow ignores `gAMA` on decode, so oxipng's `--strip safe` dropped it from a gamma 1.0 PNG, the pick passed as identical, and Chrome rendered it up to 73/255 lighter. Lossless PNG rungs keep `cICP`, `iCCP`, `sRGB`, `gAMA`, `cHRM`, `sBIT`, `pHYs` and the APNG chunks, and the identity check compares the colour chunks too. A PNG whose `gAMA` or `cHRM` decides its rendering (no `sRGB` chunk, no ICC profile) gets lossless rungs only, because pixel encoders drop those chunks; it cannot be resized or converted. The same holds for a `cICP` chunk that does not signal sRGB: browsers apply it ahead of `iCCP`, `sRGB` and `gAMA`, and Pillow does not read it at all, so imgopt reads it from the file. A linear-transfer PNG rendered as (188,137,99) in Chrome while a JPEG made from its stored samples, (128,63,31), scored SSIM 1.0 (external review, 2026-10-12).
- **APNG frames.** A lossy rung reads the first frame only, so an animated PNG once came out as a still marked `apply`. Animated PNGs get lossless rungs only, their frames are compared like a GIF's, and a resize or conversion is refused for that file.
- **SVG semantics.** svgo's `preset-default` removed `role="img"` and every id, including the ones `aria-labelledby` points at, and the rendering check could not see it. The bundled config keeps ids, `role`, `aria-*`, classes, `<desc>` text and empty groups (which may carry an id), and does not inline `<style>` blocks; without the last two, every Sketch export lost its "Created with Sketch." description and an empty layer group and got no pick at all; a pick that loses an id, class, role, `aria-*` attribute or title/desc text, or that renders differently, is discarded.
- **Metadata is listed, not kept.** Lossless JPEG and PNG rungs strip EXIF (unless it carries the orientation), XMP, IPTC, comments and PNG text, where copyright and credit fields live. Every pick lists what it removes, in `candidates`, on the page and in the record, and the skill tells the agent to say so before applying; nothing prevents the removal.

## Picking and verdicts

- The smallest passing candidate wins. Ties go to lossless, then to progressive.
- **In-place jobs** (same format, same size) leave the file untouched when the saving is below max(1 KB, 1%): a few bytes are not worth a binary diff in a repository.
- **Resizes and format changes** always apply their pick, because the change was asked for. A resized or converted file larger than its original is still applied, but it is flagged LARGER so the human hears about it first.
- **When nothing passes**, the verdict names the closest candidate. For a resize under `high`, it also suggests `medium`: shrinking green-glass-jars from #69539 to 240 px under `high` found nothing, with the best candidate at SSIM 0.974.

## Review loop (2026-10-11)

The WooCommerce replay (2026-10-10: imgopt at `e5fda462` against WooCommerce `689f232d21b`, the trunk before any recompression PR) showed the 2.0 flow stalling once work is split into file sets. A second `candidates` run in a folder hid the first run's files, the cache lived inside each folder, a bare name selected every file with that name, and the only per-file levers were run-wide flags. The tool now runs as a loop of four steps, each with one output an agent can rely on:

| Step | Command | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Optimize a file set | `candidates` | every measured variant per file, and a default choice per file (the smallest variant that passes the profile) |
| 2. Human review | `sheet`, then `record` | a page; the human's verdicts pasted back and stored in the working folder |
| 3. Placement | `manifest`, or the optional `apply` | paths: for each file, the chosen variant on disk and its target |
| 4. Other variants | `variants`, `choose`, then `sheet` again | the human's or agent's choice per file, shown on a fresh page |

The profile stays the quality bound: it decides the default choice (`pick` in the record, a file name: a pick belongs to its record), and the human can overrule it per file (`choice`, the variant's key: a choice outlives re-runs, so it names the variant itself, as verdicts do). A gate-failing variant is only ever chosen on the human's word, and it is labelled wherever it shows.

- **Verdicts travel as a pasted block.** The page is a static file and cannot write anywhere. "Copy verdicts" gives one text block (a header line, then JSON with the page's fingerprint, its working folders and a verdict, variant and note per file keyed by source path), the human pastes it to the agent, and `record` writes `verdicts.json` into each working folder. The block names its folders because the first version did not: `record` had to be told them again, and the `choose` and `sheet` lines it printed named one folder of a two-folder page, so the rebuilt page lost half its files (found in the 2026-10-11 acceptance run). `record` with no folders now uses the block's, and refuses folders that differ. `verdicts.json` holds the human's work, so it is written whole (a temp file and a replace) and a corrupt one is refused rather than read as empty. That is the bench's earlier review-record idea promoted into the tool; the bench now writes its own result to `acceptable.json`, because a bench review folder is also an imgopt working folder, where `verdicts.json` is the page's verdict store. A local server stays out of scope until the paste proves to be a problem. `apply --approve` went before 2.0.0 shipped, so there is one way to say what the human accepted.
- **A fingerprint, because a page goes stale.** The fingerprint hashes each file's source hash, its current choice and the variants the page offered, each variant named by its key (its content), not its label. A label survives a re-run with other settings: in the final review a keep for the 800 px `cjpegli-q85` (179,990 B) was accepted for the 600 px one (81,763 B) after a `--resize` re-run and a `choose`. Stored verdicts carry the key too, and a re-run keeps a file's choice only while its key is still measured; before, a re-run silently dropped the choice. A verdict from a page built from other results (a `choose` or `candidates` ran since) would land on a variant nobody saw, so `record` refuses the block. For the same reason a stored verdict stops counting once its file's choice changes. `record` validates every named variant before it changes anything: a block applied halfway would move the fingerprint and could not be pasted again.
- **A page refuses a file with results in two of its folders.** The page, the block and `verdicts.json` key files by source path, so a second result would shadow the first verdict and the file could be placed twice. This departs from the spec: a one-file set (`--assume-srgb`, a `--resize`) is reviewed on its main set's page only when the main run left that file out.
- **A verdict names the variant on screen.** The page sends the shown variant with every verdict. Switching a file's variant clears its verdict (the note stays), so a lossless choice's pre-filled keep cannot follow a browse to a lossy variant. A keep, milder or stronger on a variant other than the current choice chooses it first, so "stronger" means stronger than what the human was looking at. Reject ignores the variant. The selector always names the variant on screen: with no choice the first listed is shown and sent, because the first version selected nothing while the browser showed that variant, and a keep went out as `variant: null`. The 1:1 crops exist only for the choice the page was built with; crops for another variant need `choose` and a new page. A card switched to another variant labels the status line as the page's choice's (it was worked out for the choice) and adds the scores of the variant on screen. A card whose variant on screen a keep would write, and which has no crops of it, says so: a switched card, or a lossy pick the page saw untouched under the saving floor, which a keep now writes by hand. `record` names each such keep with the `sheet` command to view its crops, because a keep otherwise skipped the required 1:1 review. A rebuilt page starts from the verdicts already recorded. The acceptance run found both gaps.
- **One meaning of accepted.** A lossy choice needs a keep; a lossless choice needs no verdict or a keep; reject, milder and stronger hold either back. One predicate (`review.is_accepted`) decides it for `apply` and `manifest --accepted`, after the first version of `manifest` listed lossless files that `apply` held back. `apply` refuses the whole run when lossy choices exist and none has any verdict, the likeliest sign that `record` was skipped.
- **A keep writes, or says why not.** A keep of a variant under the saving floor (`--min-saving`, or 1 KB and 1%) used to be stored and then dropped by `apply` in silence. The floors stop only the gates' own pick: a variant the human chose (a keep on the page, `choose`, or a choice carried through a re-run) is written whenever it is smaller than its original. A by-hand choice of the gates' own pick is stored as the choice too: a re-run used to drop it, since only a choice that differed from the pick was stored, and the kept pick fell back under the floor. `record` refuses a keep that names no variant, and `record` and `apply` name any keep that still writes nothing, such as a variant no smaller than an in-place original.
- **A shared variant store.** Each measured variant goes into a content-addressed store at `<cache root>/store`, keyed like the old per-folder cache (source and reference hashes, settings, rung, tool identities). A run in any working folder looks there first, so the triage run over everything pays for every later set: one folder per set, with no encoding. Folders hardlink their files from the store and copy where a link fails, so variant files are write-once: the encoder and `restore` unlink a file before replacing it, and the skill says to copy a variant, never move or hard-link it. A file of the same name already in a folder counts as the stored variant only when it is the same file or has the same bytes: a re-run with other settings in one folder used to keep the old run's file under a record describing the stored one (57,473 B in the record, 10,036 B on disk in the final review). `candidates` ends with how many variants came from the store and how many it encoded, which the acceptance run could only infer from timings. Both retention limits are constants: variants unused for 30 days go, then the least recently used until the store holds under 2 GB. No retention figure had been recommended, so these are the maintainer's ruling. Store writes are best effort, so a sandbox that cannot write the cache runs without it.
- **The produced-variant index replaces `written.jsonl`.** Before, `apply` appended the hash of each lossy pick it wrote to `written.jsonl` under the cache root, and `candidates` refused those files. A file an agent copied into place itself never reached that record. The store now indexes the sha256 of every lossy variant it holds, with its kind and the source's hash, so a lossy run refuses any such file however it got into place. The index is never pruned: a variant evicted from the store can still sit in a repository, and forgetting it would let a second lossy pass stack on the first. It skips a variant that lost nothing (the source's or the reference's own bytes, or pixel-identical output): the first index (`produced/`) marked every variant of kind lossy, including a rung that handed back the source's bytes, so that untouched original was refused for good, `--ref-rev` included (found in the acceptance run; 7 of the 2,091 entries in the maintainer's store). 2.0.0 was unreleased, so the store's prune deletes that directory and the new index (`outputs/`) starts clean.
- **`apply` stays, as the verifying helper.** `manifest` is the contract for an agent that places files itself, and a copy checks nothing. `apply` re-measures each choice beside its target before moving it into place, refuses to clobber a file it is not optimizing, and stops when ffmpeg or ssimulacra2 changed since the measurement.
- **Exact file selection.** `apply --only`, `apply --files` and the `--file` of `variants` and `choose` take an absolute path, a relative path or a unique bare name, and refuse an ambiguous name with the matching paths.
- **`manifest` reads as a copy instruction.** Its text line prints `variant -> target`; it printed `source -> variant`, so for a format conversion an agent following the arrow would have copied a JPEG over the `.png`. The metadata a choice removes shows in `variants`, `choose`, `apply` and `manifest` (`metadata_removed` in its JSON), where the acceptance run found it only on the page.
- **`--assume-srgb` for one checked file.** A PNG with `gAMA` or `cHRM` and no `sRGB` chunk or ICC profile still gets lossless candidates only by default. The human can compare such a file in a browser and allow lossy candidates for it, run as a one-file set, so the cautious rule stays the default for every other file.

Evidence from the replay:

- **The in-use sets landed within a few KB of what shipped.** One `high` run over the 373 images WooCommerce ships gave 168.9 KB for the in-use pattern placeholders (164.7 KB shipped), 119.2 KB for the mobile app modal (116.8 KB), 44.1 KB for the payment logos (41.4 KB) and 101.7 KB for onboarding (100.1 KB); the block editor previews and the Salesforce icon matched exactly. The payment and onboarding gaps are `paypal.png` (banding 4.0 on a flat logo, against a gate of 3) and `logo-tiktok.png` (gamma-tagged, so lossless only): the two cases that per-file verdicts and `--assume-srgb` now cover.
- **28 basenames repeat among the 373 shipped images** (`mastercard.svg` four times), so a bare name that selected every match was unsafe; it is now refused when ambiguous.
- **The top 30 of 83 picks held 80% of the 1,166 KB saving** (the top 5 held 32%, and 24 picks saved under 3 KB each). That is why `candidates` prints the savings lines, and why the skill measures the whole set before researching usage: usage research is the expensive step, and it is only worth doing for the files that carry the saving.
- **What ships came first.** `inspect` found 89% of the lossless headroom (4.0 of 4.5 MB) in docs screenshots that never ship, and 0% on `table-wood-house`, a 232 KB JPEG among the orphans whose lossy pass in #69540 saved about 1 MB. Hence the skill's first question (what ships) and its limit that `inspect` reports lossless headroom only. The run's two font-definition SVGs failed the render check, because `rsvg-convert` finds no dimensions in them.

## Tooling policy

Missing required and quality tools stop the work instead of degrading it silently. Only a tool the human explicitly declines can be skipped, with `--allow-missing`, and every output repeats the waiver. Optional tools (butteraugli) never block. jpegli's `cjpegli` is required wherever the ladder runs it and cannot be waived. The scripts never install anything.

- **jpegli is required for lossy JPEG.** On the benchmark corpus (2026-10-10) it was the most-picked lossy JPEG encoder, 121 of 231 picks under `high`, and removing it cost 4.6 to 15 points under `high` (phone uploads 30.9% to 15.9%) and 3.4 to 8.7 under `medium`, more than any tool `--allow-missing` can waive. It was optional before the corpus, when its measured gain was 1.7 points on 31 WooCommerce photos. It has no package, so building it (git, cmake and a C++ compiler) is the price of every first lossy JPEG job; `doctor` prints the build line. The build `doctor` prints is self-contained: jpegli static, libpng bundled, and the OpenEXR, GIF and JPEG readers off, since imgopt only feeds it PNG. A plain `-DBUILD_SHARED_LIBS=OFF` build still loaded five Homebrew libraries, so a `brew upgrade` could break it. `doctor` rejects a `cjpegli` that cannot start. Shipping a prebuilt binary was considered and declined.
- **Tools are identified by their build.** A tool that prints no version (cjpegli, ssimulacra2, butteraugli) is identified by a short sha256 of its binary, which keys the cache and appears in the `tools:` line. Keying on size and mtime made a copied binary invalidate every cached candidate and left the evidence unable to name the build. `apply` compares the recorded ffmpeg and ssimulacra2 identities first and refuses lossy picks when either changed, instead of failing the tight re-measurement tolerance as a MISMATCH.
- **Encoder refusals are cached.** An encoder that exits with an error status on unchanged inputs (refusing a CMYK JPEG, say) is not retried on the next run. A death by signal, such as an out-of-memory kill, is retried.
- **No browser.** `sheet --browser` and its Chrome dependency were removed. The screenshot was a fixed viewport of scaled panes, the kind of image that hid banding in the session, and Chrome could not start inside the Codex sandbox. The page carries the 1:1 crops instead and opens at 100%.

- Only mozjpeg `jpegtran` and `cjpeg` are used. A libjpeg-turbo build made files 1.5% to 4% larger.
- oxipng 10 or newer is required, so the oxipng 9.0.0 bundled with ImageOptim is refused. On the benchmark corpus oxipng 9 made the zopfli rung about ten times slower on 1.6 MP illustrations (79 s against 8.5 s per file) for 0.2 points against 1.1; plain `-o max` gives the same sizes in both.
- svgo 4 or newer is required. svgo 3's defaults drop `viewBox` and `<title>`, which the rendering check cannot see.
- Homebrew's tools (found on `PATH`) come before the copies bundled with ImageOptim.app, which date from 2023 and update only with ImageOptim; `doctor` suggests the Homebrew install when only a bundled copy is found. jpegoptim is the exception: the bundled build is linked to mozjpeg, Homebrew's to libjpeg-turbo (Homebrew keeps mozjpeg keg-only because it ships a `libjpeg` of the same name, so no formula links it). On 12 corpus JPEGs under `high` (2026-10-12), Homebrew's jpegoptim lost 4 of the 5 picks the mozjpeg build won and the picks came out 1.0% larger in all; its best passing variants were 3.3% larger. jpegoptim 1.5.6 built statically against mozjpeg 4.1.5 made the same picks as the bundled 1.4.4 on the same mozjpeg, and across 260 outputs of the ladder's settings the two differed by 0.002% in total, so the library decides, not the jpegoptim version. Without ImageOptim, `doctor` names a jpegoptim not linked to mozjpeg (it looks for mozjpeg's version string in the binary or a JPEG library beside it) and prints that static build; it does not block, because jpegoptim then mostly loses to cjpegli. Note that jpegoptim 1.5 writes baseline JPEGs unless told otherwise, where 1.4.4 kept mozjpeg's progressive default; the ladder passes `--all-progressive`, so both builds write progressive files.
- The scripts run under plain `python3`, not `python3 -I`, because Pillow may live in user site-packages.

## Running conditions

- **Working folder.** `imgopt workdir <task>` returns a folder under the cache root: `$IMGOPT_CACHE` when set, else `~/Library/Caches/imgopt/work/<task>` on macOS, else `$XDG_CACHE_HOME/imgopt` or `~/.cache/imgopt`. It survives sessions, so a re-run reuses candidates and an interrupted run resumes after its last finished candidate. A sandbox that cannot write there (Codex) gets `$TMPDIR/image-optimization/<task>`; imgopt never picks `/tmp` itself. The session scratchpad used before this lived under `/private/tmp` and changed with every session, so the cache never carried over.
- **Never in the repository.** `candidates` refuses an `--out` inside an input folder (a later folder run would optimize the working files) or a git work tree (one `git add -A` from a commit), and writes a `.gitignore` of `*` into the folder. A folder in imgopt's own cache is exempt from the git check, since that `.gitignore` already keeps it out of any commit: a home folder that is itself a git work tree (dotfiles) holds the cache, and refusing it sent the human back to the same folder. Folder inputs skip imgopt working folders (recognised by the marker below, never by file names), git-ignored files and vendored trees.
- **Only marked folders are deleted.** `candidates` writes a `.imgopt-workdir` marker into `--out`, and refuses an existing non-empty folder without it. `imgopt clean <dir>` deletes only a marked folder; `--keep-picks` keeps each record, its reference, its source copy, its pick and its choice, which is all `sheet` and `apply` read. File names such as `run.json` are common elsewhere, so a guard on them would have let `clean` delete a project folder.
- **Footprint per input.** Measured on a 238 KB, 4.2 MP WooCommerce photo (`du -sh` of the working folder, before `sheet`): under `lossless` 19 MB before the audit fixes and 2.9 MB after; under `high` 25 MB before and 11 MB after, 2.7 MB after `clean --keep-picks`. The pixel dumps are written only when a rung reads them, and the flattened reference once per file. `candidates` prints the folder's size and the `clean` command at the end.
- **Time and memory.** `candidates` measures two files at a time by default (`--jobs`), and within a file up to three encoder families (and single rungs such as oxipng) at once, each family descending in order. Without that, a set took as long as its largest file whatever `--jobs` said: in a nine-file sample a 12 MP photo alone was the whole 159 s of a nine-job run. Where the process pool cannot start, as in some sandboxes, it says so and measures one file at a time. In-place candidates that are not smaller than the original are not measured, and butteraugli runs on the pick only. Each encoder family stops descending after two levels in a row fail the gates on measured scores; errors and unmeasured discards neither count nor reset, and a variant chosen by hand is measured anyway, without restarting the descent below it. Simulated on 809 recorded benchmark files (2026-10-12), stopping after two failures skipped 45% of the rungs and changed 3 picks by 18 KB in all (no verdict changed); after one failure it skipped 53% but changed 21 picks by 608 KB, worst +38% on one photo, mostly through single-rung SSIM dips. The cost is fewer `--stronger` steps: a median of 4 variants below the pick instead of 15. On three public WooCommerce test files (two photos and a PNG) under `high`, the run went from 203 s to 158 s with identical picks; guetzli, then in the ladder, spent about 150 s of that on the 4.2 MP photo. Two jobs double the peak memory; `--jobs 1` halves it.

## Encoder choice (benchmark, 2026-10-08)

Measured with imgopt's own metrics and floors on WooCommerce files at `689f232d2`: the 31 photos in `pattern-placeholders/` (1.66 MB, the #69539 set and its siblings) and the 13 PNGs of #69553 and #69556. For each file the smallest passing candidate counts, over the settings the ladder shipped then (jpegoptim and `cjpeg` 95 to 40, jpegli 90 to 25, guetzli 84 and 90).

| JPEG ladder | `high` | `medium` |
|---|---|---|
| jpegoptim + guetzli (before) | -34.6% | -60.4% |
| jpegoptim + jpegli | -34.1% | -61.0% |
| jpegoptim + jpegli + guetzli (2026-10-08) | -36.3% | -61.1% |
| mozjpeg `cjpeg` + jpegli + guetzli (no jpegoptim) | -36.0% | -60.9% |

- **jpegli joins guetzli rather than replacing it.** Under `high` it was the smallest passing encoder on 13 of 31 photos, in about 30 ms per encode against guetzli's 19 s, but each wins photos the other loses. guetzli also refused 2 of the photos outright at both settings (non-YUV input), and its upstream is archived.
- **guetzli was dropped after the benchmark corpus (2026-10-10).** Removing it cost at most 0.5 points with jpegli in the ladder, and 1.0 without, for minutes per encode, about 300 MB of memory per megapixel and an archived upstream.
- **jpegoptim stays.** Without it the full ladder is 0.3 points larger under `high` and 0.1 under `medium`; in a ladder without jpegli, replacing it with mozjpeg `cjpeg` costs 0.8 points under `high` (-34.6% against -33.8%), and under `medium` it is the smallest encoder on 25 of 31 photos. That keeps the one reason to look in the ImageOptim bundle first.
- **PNG:** oxipng 10.2.1 and 9.0.0 give the same sizes (131,285 against 131,306 B). Zopfli mode saves 1.8% more on these small PNGs, but on 5 to 7 megapixel screenshots it took 110 to 196 s for 0.4% or less, so it runs only up to 2 megapixels (again the encoded size), and `inspect`, which has no cache, skips it. oxipng 9, the bundled copy, ran zopfli 5× slower than oxipng 10 on the small PNGs, which led to requiring oxipng 10. As a post-pass on palette candidates it saved 0.7% at 15× the time, so the post-pass stays plain. Its level stays `-o max`: on 1,818 pngquant outputs from 130 corpus PNGs (2026-10-12), `-o 4` took 40% less post-pass time but made the likely picks 0.35% larger (worst +4.0%, a photo master), and `-o 2` or `-o 3` cost 0.7 to 1.3% (2 to 5% on screenshots, illustrations and icons). With early stopping the post-pass runs on about half as many outputs, so the time `-o 4` would save is small next to bytes every visitor downloads. The benchmark corpus (oxipng 10.2.1, 2026-10-10) kept the cap: under it zopfli took at most about 35 s per file and saved 1.1 points on illustrations and 3.6 on icons; above it, synthetic 5 MP screenshots took 80 to 115 s for 0.9 to 3.3%, and photographic PNGs gained 1% at best and came out larger on 4 of 11.

## Known gaps

- **Linux** is untested. The apt package names in `doctor` are best effort, and the scripts have only run on macOS.
- **16-bit images** are refused per file: Pillow clips 16-bit gray at 255 and reads 16-bit colour as 8-bit, so counts and comparisons would mislead.
- **AVIF banding and floors** are untested: the benchmark corpus has no AVIF job, so AVIF output stays labelled uncalibrated.
- **Quality descents are measured to the bottom.** Stopping a descent at its first failure was rejected because the gates are not monotonic in quality: on the `gallery-6` test photo, cjpegli at q45 measured SSIM 0.9794 and at q50 0.9780, and q35 measured higher than q40. A descent cut at its first failure could miss a smaller passing setting below it.
- **Gamma-tagged PNG exports count as gamma-dependent.** A PNG with a `gAMA` chunk and no `sRGB` chunk or ICC profile gets lossless rungs only, including the common 0.45455 export, which costs pngquant on those files. No corpus run is planned to test whether that value counts as sRGB, so the cautious rule stays the default; `candidates --assume-srgb` lets the human allow lossy candidates for one file they compared in a browser.
- **`--ref-rev` is all or nothing.** It stops the run when any input is an SVG or did not exist at the revision, so a folder that mixes SVG and raster files cannot use it; pass the raster files by name.
- **The `medium` JPEG ladder stops at jpegoptim `-m40`.** Under `medium`, 15 of 31 photos landed on that last setting; going down to `-m20` would save about 0.8 points more. The benchmark corpus found the same (26 of 104 jpegoptim picks at `-m40`), and the stop stays at 40 by decision.
